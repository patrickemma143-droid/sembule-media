# Website copy rewrite: behavioural framing

Draft for review by Hudson (copy owner) and Tony (client) before launch. The brief says copy comes from Hudson, so treat this as a proposal, not final copy.

## The thinking

The approach borrows from Rory Sutherland's work on behavioural economics in marketing. Five ideas drive almost every line:

1. **People buy the outcome, not the equipment.** Nobody wants a PA system; they want to be heard. Lead with the job the client is hiring us for, then mention the kit.
2. **Fear of a bad day outweighs hope of a great one.** An event organiser's real worry is the dead microphone in front of the minister, or the stream that drops while relatives abroad are watching. Copy that names the risk, and shows we have planned for it, sells harder than adjectives.
3. **Reframe who the service is for.** A livestream is not for the people in the room. It is for the grandmother in London and the board member on the road. A wedding film is for the couple, who saw a fraction of their own day.
4. **Make the first step tiny.** "Check your date" asks for one fact. "Start an enquiry" sounds like paperwork. Telling people they don't need a full brief removes the reason to delay.
5. **Specific beats superlative.** No "best", "world-class" or "premium". Concrete details (the back row, the worst five minutes, one crew instead of five suppliers) are more believable.

## Claims to confirm before launch

Everything below is implied by the new copy. If any is not true, soften that line.

- The crew does a **sound check** before guests arrive (Home services intro, Services heading).
- Sembule **talks to the event team before the day** (About values). The old copy already said this.
- **Formats, timing and scope are agreed up front** (About values). The old copy already said this.
- The listed **client logos have given permission** (Home clients line, "have trusted us").
- Livestream recordings are **handed to the client after the event** (Services, livestreaming).
